Q-Point (Quiescent Point) in Transistor Circuits

Meaning of Quiescent

Quiescent means "at rest" or "inactive state" in English. It refers to a condition where a system is powered ON but not processing any input signal.

In electronics, quiescent means the DC steady state of a circuit when no AC signal is applied.

Quiescent in LDO / Linear Regulators

In an LDO (Low Dropout Regulator) or linear regulator, quiescent current (Iq) is the small current consumed by the regulator itself to operate internal circuits, even when there is no load or very small load.

  • LM / LDO meaning: Quiescent current is the standby current drawn by the regulator IC.
  • Importance: Lower quiescent current = better battery life in portable devices.

Q-Point (Quiescent Point) in Transistor Circuits

The quiescent point (Q-point) is the steady-state DC operating point of a transistor circuit when no AC input signal is applied.

Key Differences Between BJT and MOSFET Q-Points

Feature BJT Q-Point MOSFET Q-Point
Control Parameter Base Current (IB) Gate-to-Source Voltage (VGS)
Current Collector Current (IC) Drain Current (ID)
Voltage VCE VDS

BJT Q-Point

IC = β × IB

VCE = VCC − (IC × RC)

  • Ideal: VCE ≈ VCC / 2
  • Region: Active Region

MOSFET Q-Point

ID = ½ μn Cox (W/L) (VGS − Vth)²

VDS = VDD − (ID × RD)

  • Condition: VDS > (VGS − Vth)
  • Gate current ≈ 0

Why Q-Point Matters

  • Prevents distortion
